Customs first. Bag drop second.

At the airport, keep every registered item with its purchaser until Customs confirmation, and any inspection, has finished. Only then hand over checked bags.

New to this? Pay the full price, have the shop register the purchase, confirm export with Japan Customs as you leave Japan, then follow the shop’s instructions to get the tax back.

The whole journey

From purchase to repayment

Three parties each do a different job. Customs confirming export is not the same as the money arriving.

Read this diagram as text

You ask the shop, show your passport and pay the full tax-inclusive price. The shop or its refund provider registers the purchase to you and sets up the refund method. You keep every registered item, without consuming consumables, and carry everything out of Japan within 90 days. Japan Customs confirms export at your final departure airport, before you hand over checked bags, and inspects goods if the result is red. You then complete airline procedures and any payment step the provider requires. The shop or provider pays the tax amount minus disclosed fees by its own method and timing. You check the payment and follow up with the shop or provider.

1 Before you buy

Who can claim, and what counts?

Eligibility is individual. Each person qualifies, or doesn’t, on their own status and documents. Passport nationality alone does not settle it.

  • Ask the shop to check your status before you pay.
  • Bring the original passport. A phone photo isn’t a substitute.
  • Entering Japan through an automated gate? Ask the officer after the gate for the entry seal (landing stamp), which shops may need as proof. If you entered with Japan’s Trusted Traveler Program, its registered-user card is the evidence instead.
Read this diagram as text

Foreign nationals generally qualify as non-residents within six months of entry; ordinary holiday visitors normally hold Temporary Visitor status, and diplomatic, official and certain special landing permissions follow separate rules. Japanese nationals living overseas generally need to be within six months of a temporary return, show two or more continuous years living outside Japan, and present their original passport with accepted supporting evidence. Holding a foreign passport, travelling with eligible people, or living in Japan while holidaying abroad does not establish eligibility, and nobody should register another person’s purchases under their passport.

Supporting detail: documents for Japanese nationals overseas

Accepted evidence includes a Residency Certificate, a copy of the Koseki no Fuhyō (family register attachment), or a My Number Card credential, including the digital version, showing that the holder has moved abroad. Freshness and content requirements apply. Check the official eligible-purchaser list before relying on a document.

The minimum spend

JPY 5,000 before tax

General goods and consumables count together. Different travellers can’t pool their spending. An authorised procedure counter can combine the same person’s same-day purchases from participating stores under its own rules, so ask whether the shop participates before you pay.

Goods that can count

Clothing, watches, hobby goods, electronics, cosmetics and packaged food may qualify when sold through a participating shop and taken out of Japan. You may buy only as much as you can personally carry out.

The old JPY 500,000 ceiling for consumables and the general/consumable category split have been abolished. That doesn’t override airline baggage limits or the import rules of Taiwan or the Philippines.

Outside this goods-export process

  • Hotel stays, restaurant meals and transport
  • Gold or platinum bullion and gold or platinum coins
  • Items exempt from consumption tax

Our platinum rings

Jewellery is different from bullion. Ask the jeweller whether the actual sale qualifies, and whether the finished ring will be ready to carry out at departure.

2 At the till

At the shop: before and after you pay

Before you pay

  1. Ask first. Does this branch offer the November 2026 refund procedure, and do your goods and status qualify?
  2. Show your passport. The original, or the retailer’s supported method. Carry the passport even if you use a Visit Japan Web shopping code.
  3. Pay the full tax-inclusive total. Keep enough card limit or funds; the refund only comes after Customs confirmation when you leave Japan.

After paying, before you leave the shop

  1. Complete registration to the actual eligible purchaser. Sent to a separate counter? Finish its same-day procedure before it closes.
  2. Set up repayment. Ask who refunds you, which methods work, every fee, expected timing, and whether another claim step is needed.
  3. Save the evidence. Receipt, provider instructions, registration confirmation and support contact, kept where you can read them offline.

Before you walk away: a receipt alone doesn’t prove refund registration. Ask staff to confirm the purchase record and the refund setup. Show them the question cards.

2 The money

How much comes back?

The refund is the consumption tax inside the price you paid, minus any fees the shop or provider discloses. Card, bank, app or airport-cash options, currency and timing all depend on the shop.

Read this diagram as text, with both worked examples

Standard 10% example. Paid JPY 110,000. Tax inside that price: 110,000 × 10 ÷ 110 = JPY 10,000, so the before-tax price is JPY 100,000. With an illustrative JPY 1,000 fee, the estimated net refund is JPY 9,000.

Reduced 8% example. Paid JPY 10,800. Tax inside: 10,800 × 8 ÷ 108 = JPY 800; before-tax price JPY 10,000.

The reduced rate covers qualifying food and drinks, excluding alcohol and dining out. On a mixed receipt use each line’s actual rate; store rounding applies.

Payment routes vary. Confirm currency, conversion charges and any extra registration. Don’t assume airport cash is offered for every purchase.

No universal payment deadline. Write down the provider’s promised processing period. A completed Customs check doesn’t mean the money has arrived.

3 While you travel

Can we open or use it?

Consumables: keep them for export

Food, drink, medicine and cosmetics: no special sealed bag is required now, but eating, drinking or using any part of a registered consumable in Japan makes it ineligible.

Non-consumables: use can be OK

A watch or clothing may be used in Japan, as long as that item is still taken out of Japan and available for inspection.

Read this diagram as text

Record A holds packaged snacks and a watch on one receipt; Record B holds a sweater on a separate receipt. If the snacks are eaten in Japan, Record A loses its refund entirely, including the watch, while Record B can still be confirmed. Wearing the watch in Japan is fine if it is still taken out. If the sweater is left with a relative, Record B loses its refund, but Record A is unaffected. Buy everyday food separately from export shopping.

  • Buy everyday snacks separately from export shopping, on a different receipt.
  • Gifts left in Japan can’t qualify through your departure claim. Don’t leave a ring or any registered item with a relative while claiming to export it.
  • Something already missing or consumed? Go to Customs staff before using the kiosk or Visit Japan Web, and say which record is affected.

3 Packing

Pack so Customs can see it

Read this diagram as text

Until Customs confirmation and any inspection are finished, each purchaser keeps their original passport, eligibility evidence, receipts, registration confirmations, provider instructions and every registered item within reach, grouped by purchaser and then by purchase record, plus warranty or authenticity papers for any single item of JPY 1 million or more before tax. Only after that are checked bags handed over. Valuables stay in suitable cabin baggage, receipts are kept until refunds are paid, and airline and security rules still apply.

Oya leaves first. Her registered purchases travel with her to Narita on 16 November. Don’t leave any of her goods with the group continuing to Osaka.

For an expensive watch, check the model and serial details against the shop’s documents. The shop provides identifying details for an item of JPY 1 million or more; Customs may ask to see them with the item.

Shipping is a different route

Posting purchases overseas yourself is not a substitute for presenting them at departure. A shop-arranged direct export, or a shop’s direct-shipping contract with a carrier, uses a separate export exemption. Arrange that with the shop at the time of purchase.

Arriving home is a separate issue

A Japan refund doesn’t remove Taiwan or Philippine import declarations, allowances or duties. Check the destination rules for what you carry. This guide doesn’t state those allowances.

4 At the airport

The airport sequence

Use your final airport in Japan, within 90 days of purchase. Tokyo purchases can be confirmed at Kansai if that’s where you leave. This shows the order only, not a floor plan.

Read this diagram as text

First find the Customs tax-refund procedure area in the public departure area, before check-in, with the purchaser’s passport and all registered goods. Confirm export at a Customs kiosk, or through Visit Japan Web on the dedicated Customs Wi-Fi if eligible. If the result is green, meaning no inspection or Completed, Customs confirmation is finished. If it is red, meaning inspection required or Not Completed, take the passport, goods and requested documents to the inspection area and finish the inspection; red is not an automatic refusal. Only then hand over checked bags, pass security and immigration, and complete any separate payment step your provider uses.

Allow time. Queues and inspections vary. Leaving a required inspection unfinished leaves confirmation unfinished, and you can’t complete it from the gate or after leaving Japan.

How the online route works, step by step
  1. Before airport day: register passport and landing-permission details in Visit Japan Web and check you can log in.
  2. At the airport: join the dedicated Wi-Fi, log in and open Refund Procedure. Refresh after connecting.
  3. Check before confirming: have all registered goods with you. If anything is missing or consumed, go to Customs staff instead of confirming a complete set.
  4. Read the outcome: Completed means the Customs stage is finished. Not Completed means go to the inspection area with goods and documents.

Fallback: if online access fails, use a Customs kiosk or ask staff. Completed records move to History for about six weeks; payment is still separate.

Kansai checklist

At the airport

Inspection requested, or something’s wrong

Red result: inspection required

  1. Keep all your bags with you. Don’t go to bag drop yet.
  2. Take the passport, the goods and any requested documents to the Customs inspection area.
  3. Finish the inspection, then go to bag drop.

Red is not an automatic refusal. Leaving before it’s finished leaves confirmation unfinished.

An item is missing, eaten or left behind

  1. Go to Customs staff before using the kiosk or Visit Japan Web.
  2. Say which purchase record (receipt) is affected.
  3. Other complete records may still qualify.

Show staff the “item missing” card

Other problems

No purchase record, or wrong details

Allow a short transmission delay, then contact the shop. Keep the receipt and provider details. Don’t assume a kiosk can create a purchase the shop never registered.

Visit Japan Web shows no records

Check History for records already submitted, then refresh. If a purchase is still missing, contact the shop. An empty screen is not proof of clearance.

Login or Wi-Fi error

Log in again, reconnect to the dedicated network and refresh. If it still fails, use a Customs kiosk or ask staff.

Bags already handed over

Tell Customs immediately. Don’t assume the airline can retrieve them, or that goods Customs can’t inspect can still be refunded.

Running short of time

Speak to Customs or airline staff straight away. A required inspection has to finish for that refund; don’t assume it can be completed from the gate or after leaving Japan. Protect the boarding time.

5 After departure

Track the money

  1. Keep the result. Save the confirmation or History screen and the shop’s instructions. A completed Customs result is separate from payment status.
  2. Finish the provider’s steps. Card, bank, app or supported airport cash; some providers need an extra claim or account step.
  3. Check the amount against the purchase, disclosed fees and currency. Keep receipts until every claim is settled.
  4. Follow up in the right place. Once the stated processing period has passed, contact the shop or its named refund provider. Customs doesn’t run retailers’ payment helpdesks.

A simple follow-up note for each purchase

Copy these headings onto paper or a private note. The website doesn’t store them.

  • Store and date
  • Purchaser
  • Receipt or claim reference
  • Refund provider and official contact
  • Expected net refund and currency
  • Customs completion date
  • Promised processing period
  • Paid? Date and amount

Use only the shop’s or provider’s official contact channel. Share claim documents privately, and keep passport and card details out of group chats and public messages.

Japan tax-refund shopping — step by step

Checked 3 November 2026 for purchases on/after 1 November 2026. This is a family travel summary compiled from official guidance, not an official publication. Pay the tax-inclusive price first; complete export confirmation on leaving Japan; receive the refund through the shop/operator. The previous immediate tax-free checkout process does not apply to this trip's purchases. Japan Customs overview

Before shopping

  1. Check each purchaser's eligibility. Eligible foreign non-residents normally use a qualifying status such as Temporary Visitor. Japanese nationals returning from overseas have separate residence/document requirements. Bring your original passport and have the shop check your own status; a passport photo is not a substitute. Mana's confirmed attendance does not establish eligibility: living in Japan, nationality and overseas residence can affect this. Do not assume the whole family qualifies together. Official Tokyo eligibility guidance
  2. Choose a participating tax-free shop and ask before paying. The usual minimum is ¥5,000 before tax at the same shop on the same day for the eligible purchaser; do not pool different people's purchases. An authorised consolidated counter may combine qualifying shops under its rules. Only eligible goods being taken out of Japan qualify; hotel stays, restaurant meals and transport are outside this goods-export process. Bullion and tax-exempt goods are excluded. Japan Tourism Agency traveller guidance

At the shop

  1. Register the purchase while there. Present the passport or supported identification method, pay the tax-inclusive amount, and follow the retailer's tax-free registration procedure. If directed to a department-store counter, complete its same-day process before its closing time. A normal receipt alone is not a promise that an unregistered purchase can be claimed at the airport. Ask staff to confirm that the purchase record was registered to the actual purchaser. Official shop-procedure guidance
  2. Set up the refund and keep the paperwork. Follow the shop/operator's card, account, app or cash instructions. Ask about fees, net refund, supported payment method, expected timing and support contact. Save receipts, registration confirmation and operator instructions offline. For a watch/item costing ¥1 million or more before tax, have its warranty/authenticity documents available with the goods. Airport cash refunds depend on the shop/operator; never assume cash or an immediate credit. Official traveller FAQ

Questions to ask: “Is this purchase eligible under the November 2026 refund system? Is registration complete? Which operator refunds it? What will I receive after fees, how, and when? What must I do before leaving Japan?”

During the trip and when packing

  1. Keep every registered item for export. Consumables no longer need special sealed packaging, but using/eating/drinking them in Japan makes them ineligible. Keep everyday snacks/cosmetics purchases separate from export purchases. Non-consumables such as watches may be used in Japan if the actual goods are still taken out and available for inspection. If even one item in a purchase record is missing/consumed, the other items on that record also lose eligibility. Tell customs staff about consumed items rather than certifying that you still have everything. Do not mail purchases overseas yourself and assume this airport procedure will cover them. Official traveller FAQ
  2. Pack by purchaser and purchase record. Keep passports, receipts, operator details and relevant warranties accessible. Each purchaser must be able to present their own goods and take them out on their departure; do not leave Sushella's registered goods with the Osaka group. Inspectable goods may be in luggage destined for the hold, but do not hand that luggage to the airline until customs confirmation is finished. Keep valuables in suitable cabin baggage; liquids/blades still follow airline/security rules. Narita airport instructions

At the airport — before bag drop and security

  1. Go to departure tax-refund customs confirmation first. Use the final airport from which you leave Japan, within 90 days of purchase. Have your passport and all registered goods with you. A customs kiosk is the default; eligible Visit Japan Web users can instead prepare their account and use the dedicated procedural Wi-Fi in the designated public international-departure area. The VJW departure route is available for qualifying Temporary Visitor, Diplomat or Official statuses; Japanese nationals use the kiosk/staff route. An ordinary internet connection or completing arrival customs declarations does not replace departure export confirmation. If VJW is unavailable or you are not eligible to use it, use the kiosk/staff. Japan Customs online options, official traveller guidance
  2. Follow the result. Green: customs confirmation is complete. Red: take the passport, purchases and requested documents to the indicated customs inspection area; complete inspection before bag drop/security. Red means inspection is needed, not automatic rejection. Ask customs for help with missing records or an error; show the receipt/operator instructions rather than assuming the airport can create a shop registration. Japan Customs result guidance
  3. Then finish airline and departure procedures. After clearance, hand over hold luggage, complete security/immigration and reach your gate on time. If you have already checked goods into the hold, do not assume you can retrieve them for inspection. Any separate cash-refund kiosk may be elsewhere, including after security: it is a different step from export confirmation. Narita airport process
  4. Receive and track the refund. Follow any remaining shop/operator instructions and retain confirmation until payment arrives. Check the credited amount and fees; ask the shop/operator about delays. Customs confirms export but is not the retailer's refund helpdesk. Japanese export clearance does not determine destination-country import allowances. Refund methods and timing FAQ

How much comes back? — illustration

For an eligible item assumed to carry 10% tax, a ¥110,000 tax-inclusive price contains ¥10,000 tax: 110,000 ×10÷110. The tax component is ¥10,000. Net payment may be lower after operator fees; shop rounding and the actual tax rate apply. This example is arithmetic, not a watch quote or a guaranteed refund. Keep enough funds for the full tax-inclusive purchase price.
